What You Are Dealing With

Water in a Wall Cavity: Why It Lingers

A wall is a sandwich: paint, drywall, then an empty cavity with studs, sometimes insulation, and pipes or wiring. Water enters from a pipe, a window, a roof leak or a floor flood and wicks upward through the drywall paper like a paper towel dipped in a glass.

Paint hides it. The surface may look nearly normal while the bottom 12 inches of drywall and the baseboard are saturated, and the cavity behind them stays damp for days because there is no airflow in a closed wall.

Warning Signs

Signs Water Is Inside the Wall

These show up in roughly the order water works through a wall.

Bubbling or peeling paint

Moisture pushes the paint film off the drywall face. Pressing a bubble often feels cool and soft.

Soft or swollen baseboards

MDF and finger-jointed trim swell and split at the bottom edge first.

Stains that creep upward

A tide line or brown halo that grows after the surface dries shows water still wicking up.

Wet or sagging insulation

Batts that hold water lose their value and stay wet far longer than the drywall.

Musty smell near the wall

Odor with no visible stain points to a damp cavity. Do not paint or caulk over suspected mold.

Stop and Check

What to Do in the First Day

Shut off the source first. If you cannot find it, close the main water valve and watch the area. Then pull furniture a foot off the wall, remove the baseboard if it is loose and open the room so air moves.

Press a moisture meter against the drywall at several heights, 6, 24 and 48 inches, to see how far the wet reaches. Without a meter, tape a sheet of clear plastic to the wall for a day. Fog on the inside shows moisture escaping.

Do not drill holes in a wall near wiring while it is wet. If an outlet or switch is in the wet zone, leave the circuit off if it is safe to reach the panel, and call an electrician before using it again.

Decision Guide

Dry in Place or Cut It Out

Drywall that is only damp can often be saved. Drywall that has been soaked, is crumbling or touched dirty water cannot.

The Cut

Cutting Wet Drywall: How High and How Far

A flood cut removes the wet bottom of the wall in a straight horizontal line. Cut 2 feet above the highest wet reading, or higher where wicking continues, not at the visible waterline. Water travels upward beyond what you can see.

Score with a utility knife, pull the panel off in pieces and bag it. Shut off power to the area first and check for wires and pipes before the blade goes deeper than the drywall thickness. Remove wet insulation, then leave the studs and bottom plate open to air.

Studs and plates must dry before closing. Wood that reads wet on a meter can swell, warp or grow mold behind new drywall.

Mistake List

Wall Repair Mistakes That Cost You Twice

These are the usual regrets.

  • Painting or caulking over bubbled paint without opening the wall.
  • Cutting at the waterline instead of above the highest wet reading.
  • Closing the wall before studs read dry.
  • Reusing wet insulation.
  • Sealing in suspected mold with primer.

Common Questions

Wall Water Damage Questions

Can wet drywall be saved?

Sometimes. Firm drywall wet from clean water and caught early can be dried in place. Soft, crumbling or contaminated drywall must come out.

How high do I cut a wet wall?

About 2 feet above the highest wet reading, because water wicks upward. Measure with a moisture meter, not by eye.

How long does a wall cavity take to dry?

A few days with air movers and a dehumidifier in many cases, longer with insulation or heavy saturation. Readings decide, not the calendar.

Is bubbling paint always water damage?

Usually, but not always. Poor surface prep can also cause bubbles. If the bubble is cool, soft or wet, treat it as water.

Does a wall repair in a Newport Beach home need a permit?

Ask the city Building Division. Permit rules depend on scope, and the city lists permit processing and inspection scheduling on its Building Division pages.
