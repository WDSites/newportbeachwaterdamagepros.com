Coast And Neighborhoods

How The Coast Shapes Water Damage In Huntington Beach

Water damage here often arrives from outside the walls. The city's General Plan calls flooding the most common hazard in its planning area, from heavy rain, coastal storms and high tides. About 2 percent of the area sits below sea level, and some spots pond for a long time after hard rain.

City crews have answered high-tide flooding in Huntington Harbour and Sunset Beach with sandbags, raised barriers, pumps and vacuum trucks. The General Plan names Sunset Beach, the Harbour and the area south of the Pier as the highest-risk shoreline. Saltwater needs fast extraction, then drying with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers.

Inland neighborhoods such as Oakview, Five Points and Seacliff see supply lines, water heaters, roof leaks and sewer backups. Downtown gets both kinds. Its beachfront buildings sit near the Pier at Main Street and Pacific Coast Highway, where salt air and humidity help mold start after a leak.

Before We Arrive

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

A few minutes now can limit what the crew has to dry.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff for the house. If the water is coming in from the street or the tide, you cannot stop it, so move to the next steps.

Power only if it is dry

Switch off circuits to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable without stepping in water. Otherwise call the utility or 911 first.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, shoes, papers, electronics and small furniture onto counters or beds. Pull up loose area rugs if you can do it safely.

Keep people and pets out

Floodwater and sewage can carry contamination. Keep children and animals out of standing water, and avoid wading barefoot.

Take photos first

Photograph the water line, damaged items and the source before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.

Call the 24/7 line

Call (949) 670-0939 and tell us the address, the source and how far the water has spread.

Local Risks

What Huntington Beach Properties Face And What To Do About It

Each condition changes what to do in the first day.

Flood zones are common

The city says many properties sit in FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas. Homeowner policies do not cover flood, and NFIP policies carry a 30-day waiting period, so ask the Permit Center for your zone and your insurer what you have.

Harbour and Downtown surge

Cal OES lists Huntington Harbour, the area northeast of the Bolsa Chica Wetlands and the southeast corner as elevated tsunami risk, and names low Downtown as exposed. Treat surge water as contaminated and dry the structure, not just the carpet.

Settling ground

The General Plan notes elevated liquefaction risk near the coast and in the Harbour, and past subsidence between Goldenwest Street and Seapoint Street. A damp slab with no visible leak may be a cracked line, so ask for moisture mapping.

Sewer backups

The city owns about 360 miles of sewer line and 27 lift stations. A backup can start in your private lateral or the city main, so a plumber's camera check decides who gets the call.

Common Questions

Huntington Beach Water Damage Questions

Is emergency water removal available in Huntington Beach?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, with same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time. Call (949) 670-0939 and we send the closest available licensed and insured crew.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Huntington Beach?

We cover 92646, 92647, 92648 and 92649. That runs from the Pier and Downtown in 92648 to Oakview and Bella Terra in 92647, the south side near Banning Library in 92646, and Huntington Harbour and Bolsa Chica in 92649.

Does my homeowner policy cover high-tide flooding in Huntington Harbour?

Usually not. Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage, and flood coverage is a separate policy. NFIP policies carry a 30-day waiting period, so check your policy and ask your insurer. We document damage with photos, moisture logs and scope notes.

Who do I call if the water is coming from the city main?

Call the city utilities line at (714) 536-5921 first. After hours, the city routes water emergencies through the Police Department at (714) 960-8825. Then call us for extraction and drying.

Do I need a permit for water damage repairs?

Drying alone usually does not, but rebuilding may. The Permit Center at 2000 Main Street says permits are needed to alter, repair or demolish a structure, and only a California licensed contractor or the owner can pull them.

Why does mold show up so fast near the beach?

Mold needs moisture, and coastal humidity slows drying. EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours.
