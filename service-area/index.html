Coastal And Inland

Different Houses, Different Water Problems

Newport Beach, Huntington Beach and Laguna Beach sit near the ocean, so storm runoff, high tides and salt air shape the work. Low lying streets can take in water during winter storms, and coastal humidity slows drying if dehumidifiers are not sized correctly.

Costa Mesa, Irvine and Tustin sit further inland. Calls there more often involve a failed supply line, a water heater, a washing machine hose, an upstairs bathroom leak or a roof leak after rain. The method is the same, but the likely source and the drying plan differ.

Storm Season

When Calls Peak

Calls rise after heavy rain, when roofs, windows and storm drains are tested all at once. Coastal streets can also take in water when a high tide meets a storm, and crews plan extra drying time for those jobs.

The flood damage restoration page explains how storm and groundwater loss is handled, including why floodwater is treated as contaminated until proven otherwise.

Ready To Call

What To Have On Hand

Have the street address and ZIP code ready, along with the time the damage started and where the water came from if you know. If you can reach the main shutoff safely, closing it limits the loss while the crew is on the way.

Take photos of the damage before moving anything. Keep your insurance policy number nearby, because the dispatcher may suggest reporting the loss early. Coverage is decided by your insurer, not by us.

Services

Which Service Fits The Problem

Standing water calls for emergency water removal. Wet materials that need controlled drying call for water mitigation. Debris, odor and sanitizing fall under water damage cleanup, and rebuilding drywall and flooring is water damage repair.

Most jobs use more than one of these in order, and the dispatcher explains the order when you call.
