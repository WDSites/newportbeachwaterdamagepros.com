Who We Are

One Call, The Right Crew

Newport Beach Water Damage Pros runs the dispatch line that connects property owners with licensed and insured restoration professionals. When you call, you reach a dispatcher who asks what happened, tells you how to limit the damage, and arranges the crew and the inspection visit.

The business address is 4100 Newport Place Dr., Newport Beach, CA 92660. The line is answered around the clock because water damage does not keep office hours.

What We Handle

Services And Honest Limits

The work covers water mitigation, emergency water removal, water damage cleanup, water damage repair, flood damage restoration and mold remediation.

Some things are outside it. Restoration is not waterproofing, so recurring seepage through a foundation belongs to a different trade. Roof replacement and fire damage are not part of this service either, and the dispatcher will say so rather than take a call it cannot serve.

Standards

How The Work Is Described

Water damage jobs are described using the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and mold jobs using S520. You are welcome to ask any restoration company whether its crews work to those standards.

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and the damage is documented with photos, moisture readings and written notes so an insurance adjuster has records to review. Calls to our line may be recorded for quality and training purposes.

The Call

What Happens When You Phone In

The dispatcher starts with safety. Is anyone standing in water near outlets or a panel? Is the water clear, gray or from a sewer line? Can the supply valve or main shutoff be reached? Those answers decide how fast a crew is needed and what protective gear it brings.

Next comes a short description of the damage: which rooms, what materials, how long the water has been there and whether it came from a pipe, an appliance, a roof or a storm. The dispatcher then explains what to do while waiting, such as moving small items off wet floors and keeping fans away from suspected sewage.

The Visit

What An Inspection Covers

A visit includes a walkthrough, moisture readings and a written scope. The crew checks drywall, baseboards, flooring and cabinets with moisture meters and often a thermal camera, because water travels past the stain you can see.

The scope lists what can be dried in place, what has to be removed and what equipment will run. You receive the scope and price before work starts, so nothing is decided while standing in a wet kitchen with no information.

Before You Hire

Questions Worth Asking Any Restoration Company

Ask whether the company holds the right state license for the work, and check it on the Contractors State License Board lookup. Ask for proof of insurance, and ask how drying progress will be measured.

A good answer mentions daily moisture readings and a drying goal, not just a number of fans. Ask who will be on site, whether subcontractors are used and how changes to the scope are approved. A company that answers plainly is easier to trust than one that hurries you into a signature.

Records

Documentation For Your Insurer

Photos taken before and during work, moisture logs, equipment lists and a written scope all help an adjuster understand the loss. The crew keeps these records as part of the job.

Your policy decides what is covered, and only your insurer can say so. Sudden pipe failures are treated differently from slow leaks in many policies, so read the water damage section and ask questions early. Mold and flood coverage are often separate from a standard homeowners policy.

Timing

Why The First Day Matters

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. That window is the reason the line stays open all night and why the dispatcher pushes for an inspection the same day when crews are free.

Wet drywall, carpet pad and insulation hold moisture long after the surface feels dry. Starting extraction and drying early often limits how much has to be torn out, which keeps the repair smaller.

Mold

When Water Damage Becomes A Mold Job

If water sat for days or a leak ran inside a wall, mold may already be present. The dispatcher asks about musty odor, visible growth and how long the area stayed damp.

Small surface growth on hard materials is handled differently from growth inside drywall or insulation. Larger jobs use containment and air filtration, as described in the mold remediation page, and removal is followed by drying so the moisture does not feed new growth.
