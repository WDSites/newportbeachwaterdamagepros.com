Before We Arrive

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

Skip any step that puts you near live power or contaminated water.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff. Mesa Water District supplies most of Costa Mesa, so the meter shutoff may be the one to find.

Power down wet areas

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise stay back and call.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, electronics and paperwork to dry counters. Put foil under heavy furniture legs on wet carpet.

Keep people and pets out

Standing water from a toilet, drain or sewer line is contaminated. Keep everyone out of it until crews have extracted it.

Photograph everything

Take wide and close photos of the source and wet surfaces before anything moves. They support an insurance claim.

Call the 24/7 line

Dial (949) 670-0939. Tell us the street, the ZIP, what failed and whether sewage is involved.

Local Risk Factors

The Water Problems Costa Mesa Homes Actually Have

The city's 2022 Local Hazard Mitigation Plan and the sanitary district's system data point to these patterns. Each changes what you do first.

Old pipes and old roofs

The plan counts more than 4,000 properties built before 1954. Galvanized and cast iron lines leak slowly, so a stained ceiling deserves a moisture meter reading, not paint.

Sewer laterals from the 1950s

The district's first sewer dates to 1953 and is still in use. Mid-century laterals suffer root intrusion, so a slow tub drain can end as a floor-wide backup.

Pump station trouble

The district runs 20 pump stations lifting wastewater out of low areas. If a low area backs up in a storm, call the district, then us for extraction.

Flood is a separate policy

The plan places the city within FEMA hazard zones along the Santa Ana River. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so check yours and ask your insurer.

Not a tidal town

The plan skips coastal flooding because the city has no tsunami zones and its coast-facing areas sit on bluffs. Expect storm drains, roof leaks and plumbing failures.

Housing Context

Renters, Owners And Who Does What After A Loss

Only 39.6 percent of Costa Mesa housing units are owner-occupied (Census, 2020 to 2024), so most losses involve a tenant, a landlord and often a property manager. Tenants should report a leak in writing and photograph their own belongings. Landlords should call fast, because water in a shared wall does not stay in one unit.

Owners should keep a documented drying record, and we log moisture readings and scope notes you can hand to your insurer. Before plumbing or structural repair starts, ask the Building Safety Division whether a permit applies.

Costa Mesa Water Damage FAQ

Is emergency water removal available in Costa Mesa?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, with same-day response when available and no arrival time promised on the phone.

Call (949) 670-0939, give the address and say what failed. Shut off the source and keep people out of contaminated water while you wait.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Costa Mesa?

We cover 92626 and 92627. Many Costa Mesa calls come from near City Hall and the sanitary district office on the 92626 side. If your address is on the 92627 side or you are unsure, give us the street and we confirm it on the call.

Does Mesa Water District serve my address?

Mesa Water District supplies most of Costa Mesa, but not every address. Check your water bill to see who supplies yours. For a shutoff or supply question, call your water provider.

My sewer backed up. Who is responsible?

The Costa Mesa Sanitary District maintains the mains, and the lateral from your house is generally the owner's. Call the district to learn where the blockage is, treat the water as Category 3, and call us for extraction.

I rent in Costa Mesa. What should I do after a leak?

Tell your landlord or property manager in writing, photograph your belongings and check your renters policy. We document moisture readings for whoever files the claim.

Is flood damage covered by my homeowners policy?

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, which is a separate policy. Sudden and accidental plumbing damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Do I need a permit for repairs after water damage?

It depends on the work. The Building Safety Division at 77 Fair Drive handles permits and its page does not say which water repairs need one. Call (714) 754-5273 before plumbing or structural work starts.
