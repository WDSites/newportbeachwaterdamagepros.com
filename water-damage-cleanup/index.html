What Gets Cleaned

What the first 48 hours of cleanup involve

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Cleanup is built around that window.

Debris and soaked materials out

Wet carpet pad, ruined drywall sections, trim and loose contents are bagged and removed so they stop holding moisture.

Surface cleaning

Hard surfaces are scrubbed with detergent and water and then dried, which is the EPA's basic method for non-porous materials.

Sanitizing

An antimicrobial is applied to surfaces that touched Category 2 or 3 water, such as framing, concrete and sealed floors.

Odor control

HEPA air scrubbers filter the air, and deodorizing treatments are used after the source of the smell has been removed.

Contents sorting

Furniture, boxes and clothing are sorted into clean, restorable and discard piles, with photos for your records.

Contaminated Water

Sewage and storm water are not a do-it-yourself job

The EPA says to call a professional for sewage or other contaminated water. Wear gloves, an N-95 respirator and goggles without ventilation holes if you must be in the space for any reason, and do not routinely use bleach or other biocides. Do not paint or caulk over mold if you find it.

Crews working Category 3 jobs set up containment barriers, run HEPA air scrubbers and dispose of contaminated materials separately. After the area is cleaned, drying continues, and if mold shows up later the next step is mold remediation, which follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard. Health questions about exposure belong to a physician.

Your Options

Clean it yourself, or bring in a crew?

A homeowner can usually handle a small clean-water spill on a hard floor with a mop and a fan. The picture changes with dirty water, wet insulation, soaked pad or anything behind a wall.

  • Do it yourself: works for a small clean-water spill, but no readings and no disposal of contaminated material.
  • Cleanup crew: removes debris, sanitizes and deodorizes with proper protective gear and documents what was done.
  • Crew plus emergency water removal: the right call when standing water is still in the house.

If a rebuild follows, ask the city Building Division whether your repair needs a permit, and check the contractor's license on the state licensing board's lookup.

Common Questions

Water Damage Cleanup Questions

Do you take water damage cleanup emergency calls at night or on weekends in Newport Beach?

Yes. Dispatch is 24/7 and a same-day response is available when crews are free. No arrival time is promised, and the dispatcher explains what to do while you wait.

What is the difference between water removal and water damage cleanup?

Removal pumps out standing water. Cleanup deals with what the water left: debris, soaked materials, contamination and odor.

How soon should cleanup start?

As soon as the water is stopped. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

Can my carpet be saved?

If the water was clean and caught early, often yes, though the pad is usually replaced. If the water was sewage or storm water, carpet is usually discarded.

Is sewage cleanup safe to do myself?

No. The EPA says to call a professional for sewage. Stay out and call the utility or 911 if there is a gas or electrical risk.

What does an inspection visit cover?

A walkthrough, moisture readings and a written scope. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and photos and moisture logs support an insurance claim.
